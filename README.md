# Proposed Residential Development in Hatfield

## A GIS-Based Urban Planning and Spatial Analysis Project

**Study Area:** West of Hatfield Town Centre, Hertfordshire, United Kingdom
**Software:** QGIS
**Project Type:** Fictional Residential Development Planning
**Analysis:** Land Use, Accessibility, Service Proximity, Buffer Analysis and Residential Density

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project presents a fictional residential development proposal within an undeveloped area west of Hatfield town centre in Hertfordshire, United Kingdom.

The project demonstrates how Geographic Information Systems (GIS) can be applied to support urban planning and spatial decision-making.

The proposed development integrates residential areas with public services, green spaces and a connected road network. Spatial analysis was used to examine the existing condition of the study area and develop a proposed settlement layout.

The project consists of six main mapping outputs covering the location of the site, existing land use, proposed development, accessibility, service proximity and residential density.

---

## 2. Project Objectives

The main objectives of the project were to:

* Identify and map the proposed development area.
* Examine the existing land-use characteristics of the site.
* Develop a proposed residential land-use plan.
* Design an internal road network and assess accessibility.
* Assess proximity to essential services using a 200 m buffer analysis.
* Develop a residential density distribution based on accessibility and spatial planning considerations.
* Produce professional cartographic outputs suitable for a residential development report.

---

## 3. Study Area

The proposed development area is located west of Hatfield town centre in Hertfordshire, United Kingdom.

The site consists predominantly of undeveloped land and is surrounded by existing roads and neighbouring urban areas. The location provides an opportunity to demonstrate how GIS can support planned urban expansion.

The study area contains a mixture of existing land uses and natural features, including agricultural land, woodland, grassland, a small water body and an existing access track.

These existing characteristics were considered when developing the proposed residential layout.

---

## 4. GIS Methodology

The project was developed using QGIS.

The main GIS processes included:

1. Identification of the study area.
2. Digitisation of the proposed development boundary.
3. Examination of existing land use.
4. Digitisation of proposed residential and non-residential land uses.
5. Development of a proposed road network.
6. Identification of essential services.
7. Application of a 200 m buffer analysis.
8. Classification of residential areas according to density.
9. Cartographic design and map export.

The analysis was designed to demonstrate the practical application of GIS to residential development planning.

# 6. Key GIS Techniques Demonstrated

This project demonstrates several practical GIS skills:

### Spatial Digitisation

Creation of development boundaries, land-use areas and road features.

### Land-Use Mapping

Classification and visualisation of proposed residential and supporting land uses.

### Buffer Analysis

A 200 m buffer was used to examine accessibility to essential community facilities.

### Accessibility Analysis

The road network was designed to connect residential areas with services and surrounding infrastructure.

### Density Mapping

Residential areas were classified according to proposed development intensity.

### Cartographic Design

Maps were prepared with titles, legends, scale bars, north arrows and appropriate symbology.

# 7. Planning Considerations

The proposed development was designed around several planning principles:

* Accessibility to essential services.
* Efficient use of available land.
* Connectivity between residential areas.
* Provision of green and recreational spaces.
* Integration with surrounding roads.
* Differentiation of residential density.
* Protection of important natural features.

The development therefore attempts to balance housing provision with accessibility, environmental quality and supporting infrastructure.

---

# 8. Key Recommendations

The following recommendations are proposed:

1. Existing woodland and water features should be preserved wherever possible.
2. Dedicated pedestrian and cycling infrastructure should be incorporated.
3. Additional recreational and green spaces should be provided as the population increases.
4. Environmental, transport and utility assessments should be undertaken before implementation.
5. Further GIS analysis could incorporate network analysis, suitability modelling and population forecasting.

---

# 9. Project Limitations

This is a fictional development proposal and therefore represents a planning and GIS demonstration rather than a construction-ready master plan.

Further professional assessment would be required before real-world implementation, including:

* Detailed topographic surveying.
* Environmental assessment.
* Transport impact assessment.
* Utility infrastructure assessment.
* Detailed planning regulations and policy review.
* Population and housing-demand analysis.
* Engineering and feasibility studies.

---

# 10. Project Outcome

The project demonstrates how GIS can support the planning and visualisation of a proposed residential development.

By combining spatial data, digitisation, land-use planning, road-network design, buffer analysis and density classification, GIS provides a practical framework for analysing spatial relationships and communicating planning proposals.

The six maps provide a progression from the existing site condition to the proposed development and subsequent spatial analyses.
